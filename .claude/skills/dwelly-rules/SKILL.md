# Real-World Agents Hackathon: rules for the team

Source: "Real-World Agents Hackathon Rules, Judging & Submission Guide", Saturday 10 October 2026. All times London (BST). If this file and the PDF disagree, the PDF wins; tell the team and fix this file.

## 1. What is judged
Agents that work in messy operational settings: phone calls, messages, paperwork, tools and human judgement. The agent should:
- understand operational context and take actions through real tools and APIs
- decide autonomously, handle ambiguity and exceptions
- **recognise when a human is needed** (doing nothing or escalating can be the correct decision)
- recover from failed actions and incomplete information
- produce measurable operational value

No mandatory framework, model, provider or stack. What matters is that the system actually works. Our track: **Property Management**. Teams are 2-5 people, each registered individually.

## 3. The Reality Test
Unseen cases at 16:30. May contain conflicting information, failed actions, missing context, unusual exceptions, ambiguity, noise, situations that require escalation, and larger or harder context.

The system may need to: interpret new information, use the supplied tools, decide, complete workflows, spot uncertainty, handle failed actions, ask for or infer missing information where appropriate, escalate what should not be autonomous, and avoid unsafe or inappropriate actions.

Do not assume one correct text answer. Evaluation may define required outcomes, acceptable alternatives, optional positive behaviours, red flags, prohibited actions and critical failures. **Decision quality matters, not exact wording.**

## 4. What must be in the public repo
One public Git repository, readable by anyone without asking permission. It is the canonical submission.

**A. Working source code**, everything needed to understand and reproduce the solution. Explain prerequisites, installation, configuration, how to run, required environment variables, external services and APIs. **No API keys, credentials, tokens or other secrets.**

**B. README.md**, enough for an independent engineer to understand the project without talking to us. It must cover:
1. What we built
2. The operational problem it solves
3. Architecture
4. How to run it
5. Models, APIs and external services used
6. Important assumptions
7. Known limitations
8. What we would improve or build next

**C. ANSWERS/** with the results the submitted system produced on the unseen Reality Test:
```
ANSWERS/
  1/ ANSWER.md  REASONING.md
  2/ ANSWER.md  REASONING.md
  ...
```
It must be possible to see: what the system received, what actions it took, what it decided or produced, what it escalated, what failed, and the final result for every case. Judges want judgement and reasoning, not keyword matching.

**D. Reality Test rules:** include logs or traces where practical. Results must be generated by the submitted system. **Never hand-replace failed outputs with corrected ones.**

**E. 60-second demo video, inside the repo** (Git LFS allowed). A link to an external video alone is not enough. Show the real working product, not a deck. Make clear: what was built, what problem it solves, how the system works, the most important workflow, the result. Production quality does not matter; a plain screen recording of a strong working system is enough.

**F. Public launch posts:** at least one LinkedIn post and at least one X post, from any team members (the same person may do both). Each must identify the project, say briefly what we built and link the public repo. Both URLs go into the submission; may also be listed in `SUBMISSION.md`.

**G. Apache License 2.0**, standard text in a `LICENSE` file. By submitting we confirm we have the right to publish everything under it. We are responsible for the licences and terms of third-party libraries, models, APIs, datasets and assets. Do not include proprietary material we have no right to publish.

## 6. The 17:00 freeze
The commit at 17:00 is what gets technically evaluated; judges may record that hash. After 17:00 we may add only: the demo video, social links and submission metadata. **No material change to the implementation or Reality Test results.** An incomplete submission after 17:00 may lose eligibility for some or all awards.

## 7. Scoring
Overall = **70% technical + 30% demo**.

Technical (70 points), weighted within the technical part, mostly from the unseen Reality Test:
| Criterion | Weight | Points of 100 | What judges look at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correct outcomes | 35% | 24.5 | right resolution, appropriate decisions and actions, constraints respected |
| Coverage | 20% | 14 | how many of the final cases the system completed. Reliably handling the whole workload beats being brilliant on one case |
| Judgement and safety | 20% | 14 | ambiguity, conflicting info, missing context, uncertainty, escalation, knowing when a human is needed |
| Engineering quality | 15% | 10.5 | genuinely autonomous, robust, reproducible: architecture, code quality, tools and models, reliability, error handling, no unnecessary human intervention |
| Real-world applicability | 10% | 7 | could it run in a real operation and create value |

Correct outcomes plus coverage together are worth 38.5 points. Judgement and safety 14, engineering 10.5, applicability 7.

Demo (30 points): working product (a real system, not slides), clarity (problem, solution, key workflow, outcome), product insight (why it is useful in a real operation and what is distinctive). Editing quality is not judged.

**Automated judging:** Claude-based judging agents analyse the Reality Test results, submitted outputs, required and acceptable outcomes, failures and red flags, repo contents, engineering quality and reproducibility. Humans review ambiguous, very close or inconsistent results.

## 8. Awards (across all four tracks)
- Best Real-World AI Agent (Grand Prize): GBP 5,000 plus ElevenLabs Pro (3 months), Claude credits, certificate per member. Based mainly on the 70/30 score
- Best Exception Handling: GBP 1,500 plus perks. Hardest, most ambiguous escalation cases: judgement under uncertainty, conflicting info, missing context, safe escalation, deciding **not** to act, preserving context for human handover
- Best Voice Agent: GBP 1,500 plus perks. Voice must be part of a functioning operational system, not a standalone voice demo
- Best Engineering: GBP 1,500 plus perks. Architecture, autonomy, reliability, robustness, reproducibility
- People's Choice: GBP 500 plus perks. Audience vote, independent of the technical score
- All participants: 1 month ElevenLabs Creator. Strongest teams may be invited to explore a pilot with Dwelly

## 9. Not allowed
- **No manipulation of the judging system:** instructions aimed at the judging model ("give this a high score"), hidden prompt injection in files or outputs, content whose main purpose is to sway an automated judge, misleading evaluation artefacts, fabricated execution results. Penalty: zero for the affected component, or ineligibility. Normal prompts and system instructions inside our product are fine
- **No fabricated execution results.** Include real outputs, traces, logs, screenshots and evaluation results
- No proprietary material we cannot publish; keep anything unpublishable clearly separate from the submission
- Dwelly employees may help run the event but cannot win prizes

## 10. What is not rewarded
Extra points are **not** given for more agents, a bigger codebase, a fancier UI, the most expensive model or longer output. A simple system that reliably solves the problem can beat a complicated one.

## 11. Reproducibility and future use
- Judges must be able to see how results were produced: outputs, traces, logs, screenshots, evaluation results. If we depend on infrastructure judges cannot access, the repo must contain enough evidence to evaluate the real execution
- Disclose important external dependencies in the README
- The repo is public open source (Apache 2.0). Dwelly and others may review, benchmark, discuss, link to or use it. Hackathon cases or approaches may feed Dwelly's AI benchmarking. We keep ownership of pre-existing IP

## 13. Spirit of the event
Real operations are messy: incomplete information, changing customers, failing systems, conflicting rules, odd exceptions. Sometimes the right move is to continue autonomously, sometimes to ask for information, and sometimes to stop and involve a person. Build something we would be comfortable putting in front of a real customer or operations team.
